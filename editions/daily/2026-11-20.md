# A cyber fine upheld puts supplier risk on the partners' desks

*The First-tier Tribunal has upheld a £60,000 Information Commission fine on DPP Law, and its deterrence reasoning points at firms that rely on external suppliers.*

[REPORTED] The First-tier Tribunal has dismissed DPP Law's appeal against a £60,000 fine from the Information Commission, then the Information Commissioner's Office, Legal Futures reports. The fine followed a 2022 cyber attack that exposed data on 682 clients.

The tribunal found the firm's failings negligent and the penalty justified as a deterrent to other law firms relying on external suppliers. DPP notified the regulator 43 days after it became aware of the breach, according to the ICO. The Law Society Gazette reported the original fine in April 2025.

The Verdict's view: The £60,000 is the least of it: a tribunal that calls the penalty a deterrent for firms using suppliers is saying outsourced IT does not outsource accountability, and the 43-day gap is the figure regulators will quote back.

[REPORTED] The European Court of Human Rights has barred a Romanian lawyer from representing applicants for two years, the Law Society Gazette reports. Submissions she drafted with AI contained non-existent cases and misused authorities, and the court had to spend considerable time verifying them. She admitted using AI tools, and the chamber found her conduct met the exceptional-circumstances threshold under Rule 44D.

The Verdict's view: A bar is a heavier sanction than a costs order, and the reasoning rests on wasted court time rather than AI use itself, so the exposure follows unchecked output, not the tool.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/lawyer-banned-from-ecthr-after-using-ai-to-draft-submissions/5128596.article

[REPORTED] Clio has asked a federal court in Virginia for a declaratory judgment that it need not reactivate a case law feed licensed to ROSS Intelligence in 2017, LawSites reports. According to the complaint, filed on 22 October, ROSS contends the agreement remains in force through 2029. Clio says it refused, and the dispute turns on whether the contract renewed after ROSS shut down in 2021.

It follows the failed Fastcase and Alexi mediation reported on 18 November: Clio is again the party asking a court what a data licence permits.

The Verdict's view: Going to court first suggests Clio expects a fight over a relaunched ROSS, and the question to carry into your own contracts is what a licence means once its supplier has closed.
