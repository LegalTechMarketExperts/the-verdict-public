# Clio widens its platform; firms may have to prove AI control

Clio's purchase of a regulatory monitoring company, reported by more than one outlet, extends a practice management platform into compliance territory, with price and terms unstated.

[REPORTED] Law360 Pulse Legal Tech reports that Clio has acquired a company offering government relations and regulatory tracking services. Legal Tech Monitor names it as Page and says it follows legislative updates and hearings across the US, UK, Ireland, Australia and Canada. Neither report gives a deal value or terms, and the scope of the target's products is unconfirmed. [VERIFY: check the deal terms and the target's name against Clio's announcement.]

The Verdict's view: a practice management vendor buying regulatory monitoring is a bid to own more of the firm's daily workflow. Before you buy tracking from a platform vendor, ask which jurisdictions and sources it covers, and what it costs beyond your current plan.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/clio-acquires-regulatory-monitoring-company-page
https://law360.com/pulse/legal-tech/articles/2542582

[REPORTED] Legal Tech Monitor reports that Clio announced a revamped platform at ClioCon 2026, including an AI phone system, a shared workspace, agentic AI drafting and a timekeeping tool in beta. It gives no pricing or availability dates. [VERIFY: confirm the feature list with Clio.]

The Verdict's view: until pricing and dates arrive, treat it as a roadmap, not something you can buy.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/clio-announces-revamped-platform-agentic-ai-drafting-additional-ai-capabilities

[REPORTED] Legal Futures looks at what the Solicitors Regulation Authority may expect firms in England and Wales to show about their control of the AI systems used to deliver legal services. It argues that AI increases scrutiny of supervision, and does not set out any specific new SRA requirement.

The Verdict's view: this is an argument about direction, not a rule, and nobody can plan against it yet.

https://legalfutures.co.uk/blog/sra-supervision-and-ai-what-firms-will-be-asked-to-prove

[REPORTED] Today's Conveyancer reports that more than 150 signatories have backed calls for the Ministry of Justice to review plans to pass part of the interest on lawyers' client accounts in England and Wales to government, for wider access to justice spending. Canadian Lawyer reports that UK firms are challenging the scheme, without giving grounds or a forum. [VERIFY: check the UK challenge and government scheme details.]

The Verdict's view: the plans are proposals, not rules, but they touch income some firms may be counting on.

https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/sme-and-high-street-lawyers-alliance-turns-its-attentions-to-client-account-proposals
https://canadianlawyermag.com/news/international/uk-law-firms-challenge-interest-on-lawyers-client-accounts-scheme/394845

[ANNOUNCED] Legatics says it has added write capabilities to its transaction management platform, letting AI tools set up and progress live deals. The announcement is dated 27 October from London.

The Verdict's view: write access turns the risk from a wrong answer into a wrong action, so ask where write actions are logged and who can reverse one.

https://legatics.com/legatics-now-lets-ai-tools-set-up-and-progress-live-deals
