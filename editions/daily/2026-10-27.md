# Clio widens its platform; firms may have to prove AI control

Clio's purchase of a regulatory monitoring company, announced by Clio itself, extends a practice management platform into compliance territory, with price and terms unstated.

[ANNOUNCED] Clio says it has acquired Page, a Kitchener-Waterloo company offering government relations and regulatory tracking services; Law360 Pulse Legal Tech and Legal Tech Monitor also carried it. Page's own site says it follows legislative updates and hearings across the US, UK, Ireland, Australia and Canada. Clio's release gives no value or terms; the deal closed in October and Page's 12-person team joins Clio, with the tracker to be built into Vincent.

The Verdict's view: a practice management vendor buying regulatory monitoring is a bid to own more of the firm's daily workflow. Before you buy tracking from a platform vendor, ask which jurisdictions and sources it covers, and what it costs beyond your current plan.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/clio-acquires-regulatory-monitoring-company-page
https://law360.com/pulse/legal-tech/articles/2542582

[ANNOUNCED] Clio announced a revamped platform at its ClioCon 2026 keynote in Boston on 26 October, including an AI phone system, a shared workspace, agentic AI drafting and a timekeeping tool in beta, as Legal Tech Monitor reports. Clio says the unified platform starts rolling out in November 2026, the phone system is on trial in some US states, drafting and the shared workspace are live for US Clio Work customers, and timekeeping is an opt-in beta; no pricing is given.

The Verdict's view: part of this is buyable now by US Clio Work customers and part is roadmap, so ask which parts reach UK firms, when, and at what price.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/clio-announces-revamped-platform-agentic-ai-drafting-additional-ai-capabilities

[REPORTED] A guest post on Legal Futures argues that the Solicitors Regulation Authority may expect firms in England and Wales to show how they control the AI systems used to deliver legal services. It argues that AI increases scrutiny of supervision, and does not set out any specific new SRA requirement.

The Verdict's view: this is an argument about direction, not a rule, and nobody can plan against it yet.

https://legalfutures.co.uk/blog/sra-supervision-and-ai-what-firms-will-be-asked-to-prove

[REPORTED] Today's Conveyancer reports that more than 150 signatories have backed calls for the Ministry of Justice to review plans to pass part of the interest on lawyers' client accounts in England and Wales to government, for wider access to justice spending. The Ministry's consultation closed in March 2026 and its lead option takes 75% of pooled client account interest. The challenge is an open letter to the Prime Minister from the SME & Boutique Law Firm Alliance, which asks for no decision while the consultation is unresolved and says it will explore legal remedies if the scheme proceeds.

The Verdict's view: the plans are proposals, not rules, but they touch income some firms may be counting on.

https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/sme-and-high-street-lawyers-alliance-turns-its-attentions-to-client-account-proposals
https://canadianlawyermag.com/news/international/uk-law-firms-challenge-interest-on-lawyers-client-accounts-scheme/394845

[ANNOUNCED] Legatics says it has added write capabilities to its transaction management platform, letting AI tools set up and progress live deals. The announcement is dated 27 October from London.

The Verdict's view: write access turns the risk from a wrong answer into a wrong action, so ask where write actions are logged and who can reverse one.
