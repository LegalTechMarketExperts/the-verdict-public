# AI is not a legal entity, so the solicitor stays accountable

The Law Society of England and Wales has reminded solicitors that AI is not a legal entity, which leaves responsibility for its output where it already sat.

[ANNOUNCED] The Law Society of England and Wales has issued a 58-page practice note, with a press release, reminding solicitors that AI is not a legal entity. The note addresses how responsibility for AI use should be understood under professional obligations. Artificial Lawyer and the Law Society Gazette carried the story. Artificial Lawyer lists the note's steps for firms: human oversight, disclosure of AI use to clients, no tool treated as automatically safe, and AI not used as a substitute for junior work.

The Verdict's view: A practice note is guidance, not a new rule, but it ends the argument that the tool decided, so every AI-assisted output needs a named person answering for it.

https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/10/21/ai-is-not-a-legal-entity-says-law-society

[CONFIRMED] Steve Smart, the FCA's executive director of enforcement and market oversight, told the Law Society Economic Crime Conference 2026 that the FCA is not looking to catch you out if you are trying to do the right thing on AML supervision, according to Today's Conveyancer. The speech updated legal firms in England and Wales on the FCA taking over their AML supervision, due to begin in late 2028 and complete by 2030; the report does not give the date of the conference. The Verdict's view: Goodwill in a speech is not a safe harbour, so keep your records ready to show good faith.

https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/not-looking-catch-says-fca-latest-update-aml-supervision-takeover

[ANNOUNCED] Everlaw says its new survey of in-house legal teams finds AI delivering results despite lingering concerns. It reports in-house lawyers gaining more control over outsourcing to law firms, firms passing AI savings to clients, and a possible shift from billable hours to billable value. The survey, run with Wakefield Research, covers 300 US in-house lawyers; fieldwork dates and sampling are not given. The Verdict's view: A vendor survey records what clients say they want; your own instruction data shows what they do.

https://everlaw.com/blog/corporate-legal/new-research-concerns-aside-ai-is-delivering-results-for-in-house-legal

[REPORTED] The Law Society Gazette reports that HM Courts & Tribunals Service worked with technology partners to restore service after a power event at Google Cloud's London zone on 20 October took down the Cloud Video Platform for much of the morning; Google reported the outage mitigated for most customers by early afternoon. HMCTS has not published a notice on affected hearings. The Verdict's view: Remote hearings are infrastructure, so a contingency plan for a missed hearing should assume the platform fails.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/power-event-disrupts-remote-hearings/5128285.article

[ANNOUNCED] Sanford Heisler Sharp McKnight has launched a dedicated AI and technology litigation practice group, announced on 20 October and reported by Law360 Pulse. The firm, a US plaintiffs practice, says the group is chaired by Palo Alto managing partner Ella Zheng and draws on 43 lawyers; its first flagged matter is a whistleblower suit against xAI. The Verdict's view: Firms organising around AI disputes signal demand for counsel before they signal demand for software.

https://law360.com/pulse/legal-tech/articles/2540214
