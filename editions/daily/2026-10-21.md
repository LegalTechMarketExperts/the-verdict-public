# AI is not a legal entity, so the solicitor stays accountable

The Law Society of England and Wales has reminded solicitors that AI is not a legal entity, which leaves responsibility for its output where it already sat.

[REPORTED] Artificial Lawyer reports that the Law Society of England and Wales has issued a practice note reminding solicitors that AI is not a legal entity. The note addresses how responsibility for AI use should be understood under professional obligations. The Law Society Gazette also carried the story. The report does not set out the note's specific steps for firms.

The Verdict's view: A practice note is guidance, not a new rule, but it ends the argument that the tool decided, so every AI-assisted output needs a named person answering for it.

https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/10/21/ai-is-not-a-legal-entity-says-law-society

[CONFIRMED] Steve Smart, the FCA's executive director of enforcement and market oversight, told a Law Society event that the FCA is not looking to catch you out if you are trying to do the right thing on AML supervision, according to Today's Conveyancer. The speech updated legal firms in England and Wales on the FCA taking over their AML supervision; the report does not give the date. The Verdict's view: Goodwill in a speech is not a safe harbour, so keep your records ready to show good faith.

https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/not-looking-catch-says-fca-latest-update-aml-supervision-takeover

[ANNOUNCED] Everlaw says its new survey of in-house legal teams finds AI delivering results despite lingering concerns. It reports in-house lawyers gaining more control over outsourcing to law firms, firms passing AI savings to clients, and a possible shift from billable hours to billable value. [VERIFY: survey sample size and methodology] The report's sample size and method are not stated. The Verdict's view: A vendor survey records what clients say they want; your own instruction data shows what they do.

https://everlaw.com/blog/corporate-legal/new-research-concerns-aside-ai-is-delivering-results-for-in-house-legal

[REPORTED] The Law Society Gazette reports that HM Courts & Tribunals Service is working with technology partners to restore service after a power event disrupted remote hearings. The report gives no timings, scale or cause. [VERIFY: restoration status and affected hearings on the HMCTS notice] The Verdict's view: Remote hearings are infrastructure, so a contingency plan for a missed hearing should assume the platform fails.
