# LLP tax scrutiny may reach law firm budgets before AI does

HMRC is reported to be testing LLP members' self-employment status after the Supreme Court's BlueCrest ruling, and PKF Francis Clark warns the tax at stake could be material for law firms.

[REPORTED] HMRC is putting professional services firms under aggressive scrutiny following the Supreme Court's BlueCrest ruling on LLP members' self-employment status, the Law Society Gazette reports. Boston Consulting Group's UK arm is reported to be among those targeted, and says it is in discussion with HMRC.

PKF Francis Clark's Andrew Allen outlines capital and remuneration steps firms are reviewing. The Gazette's headline says LLPs dodged a bullet in the Budget. The ruling is law in the UK; the scrutiny of firms is a report, not an announced HMRC programme.

The Verdict's view: a tax dispute over member status will sit beside technology spend in partner meetings, and a firm that must reserve cash for it may slow discretionary projects.

Ask your finance director what the firm has modelled on member status, and when the partners will see it.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news-focus/in-depth-llps-dodged-a-bullet-in-the-budget-but-hmrc-is-on-their-case/5128456.article

[REPORTED] Senior Costs Judge Rowley has called for a review of the electronic bill of costs, ten years after its introduction, ahead of the Association of Costs Lawyers Conference, the Law Society Gazette reports. He says the 55-column Excel format slows assessment and is hard for litigants in person to follow. He does not favour a return to paper, and the Senior Courts Costs Office is looking at the Supreme Court's Form 5 bill as a model. This is a call for review in England and Wales, not a rule change. The Verdict's view: costs teams and bill software vendors have a design brief from the bench, but a call for review is not yet a new form.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/judge-seeks-new-version-of-electronic-costs-bill/5128459.article

[REPORTED] Mr Justice Fordham has refused the London Borough of Tower Hamlets its costs of the permission hearing in a judicial review, and its costs of preparing the late documents, after its solicitor served key documents very late on a litigant in person, Legal Futures reports. The litigant, Mabel Abdul, acts for her disabled four-year-old son. The judge said the council had failed to acknowledge its defaults and that the position placed on her was intolerable. The council may not rely on the late documents. This is a decided ruling in England and Wales. The Verdict's view: costs sanctions for late service give litigation teams facing a litigant in person a reason to log every service date.
