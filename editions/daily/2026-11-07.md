# Legal AI's first listing talk is a contract question for buyers

*Harvey has sounded out its regular law firm advisers on a listing, and buyers of legal AI now have a supplier-stability question to put in writing.*

[REPORTED] Harvey has held preliminary meetings with Latham & Watkins, its regular advisers, about a possible future listing, Legal Business understands. On that account the talks were preliminary and Harvey has no imminent IPO plans. Business Insider reported the discussions first, and Legal Tech Monitor has carried them too.

Artificial Lawyer's weekly wrap adds that Legora is reported to be in early discussions with advisers as well. Legora's spokesperson said it does not comment on speculation, though chief executive Max Junestrand has long said he wants to list one day. Both accounts are reported talks, not company announcements.

The Verdict's view: Preliminary talks with the usual advisers are housekeeping, not a timetable, but two legal AI vendors linked with listings in one week suggests suppliers are being shaped for outside shareholders, and renewal terms should allow for that.

Worth doing: before your next renewal, ask what the contract says if the supplier changes owner or lists.

https://legalbusiness.co.uk/law-firms/harvey-held-preliminary-meetings-with-latham-advisors-over-potential-listing

[REPORTED] Mrs Justice Joanna Smith told the Bond Solon Expert Witness Conference that experts should declare AI use now, before a rule requires it, The Law Society Gazette reports. She co-chaired the Civil Justice Council working group whose report last week recommended a declaration where AI shaped the substantive content of an expert report. She said the duties of independence and objectivity have not changed. This is England and Wales, and the declaration is recommended, not in force.

The Verdict's view: A judge asking for disclosure before any rule exists makes it the sensible default, and the firms that instruct experts are the ones who will be asked why they waited.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/start-telling-us-now-about-ai-use-top-judge-urges-experts/5128458.article

[REPORTED] The Legal Services Board has welcomed Richard Lloyd's review of legal services regulation, which the government has accepted in full, and says change needs strategic investment, The Law Society Gazette reports. Chair Monisha Shah says the LSB will set out a 2027-30 strategy, with a budget consultation to follow. Blind Justice UK criticised the review for not covering the SRA or setting a timetable.

The Verdict's view: Since the government's acceptance, the regulator has put a price on reform, and the bill will reach firms through regulatory budgets, so that consultation matters more than the 2029 deadline.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/we-will-change-but-it-will-cost-you-says-lsb/5128451.article
