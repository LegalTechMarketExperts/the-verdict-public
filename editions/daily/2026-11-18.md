# A law firm is funding the rival that wants its job

Alecto, an AI-native conveyancing firm with Mishcon de Reya among its investors, says it reached £6m annualised revenue in eight months, a figure that is the company's own.

[REPORTED] Alecto, an AI-native property law firm regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers, has raised a £15m seed round, Legal Futures reports. Mishcon de Reya and Firstminute Capital are among the investors. Founder Will Rice says Alecto is replacing the traditional law firm.

Nonbillable's account of the round, which rests on company statements, puts it at $20m, led by Insight Partners, with $8m annualised revenue. Alecto starts with UK residential conveyancing, plans other consumer law areas and says it will launch internationally in 2027.

The Verdict's view: Mishcon is hedging, not surrendering: it buys an early look at a model whose revenue claim is still the company's own.

Before benchmarking against Alecto's run rate, ask how many completed transactions stand behind the annualised figure.

[REPORTED] A study by HumanizeMy.ai, an AI writing and detection vendor, found ChatGPT's link-tracking tag in 298 US federal court cases across 71 courts and in 133 SEC filings, LawSites and LawNext reports. The researchers searched the RECAP archive of federal filings, which is not a complete record, so the real count could be higher. Founder Firat Mihci says the tag shows only that a link was copied from ChatGPT, not that the source is wrong.

The Verdict's view: A copied link proves where a citation started, not that it failed, but it shows that unlogged AI use is already visible to anyone who searches the public record.

https://lawnext.com/2026/11/study-finds-chatgpt-tracking-tag-in-298-federal-court-cases-and-133-sec-filings.html

[REPORTED] A court-ordered mediation between Fastcase and Alexi did not settle their federal lawsuit, the parties told the court on 12 November, LawSites and LawNext reports. Fastcase, now owned by Clio, sued in December 2025 over alleged misuse of its data by Alexi, a Toronto-based AI legal research company, under a 2021 licence. The court has not yet responded to a request for a scheduling conference.

The Verdict's view: Data licences written before generative AI are now being tested, and a buyer of AI research tools should expect the supplier's data rights, not its model, to be the weak point.
