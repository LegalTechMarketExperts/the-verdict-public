# The SRA prefers supervising to investigating, and firms will pay

*The Solicitors Regulation Authority's final business plan lifts the practising certificate fee share to £240 and explores supervision in place of some investigations.*

[REPORTED] The Solicitors Regulation Authority has published its final business plan for the year to 31 October 2027, Legal Futures reports. It plans to explore alternatives to formal investigations, such as supervisory dialogue or conditions on authorisations. The practising certificate fee share rises by £50, to £240, to raise an extra £25m, with £23m for IT and change programmes.

The plan is final for firms regulated in England and Wales; the alternatives to investigation are an intention to explore, so treat them as direction, not rule. Misconduct reports rose 45 per cent to 16,499.

The Verdict's view: A regulator facing 16,499 misconduct reports is choosing triage, and firms that can show clean records on request will reach the lighter touch first.
