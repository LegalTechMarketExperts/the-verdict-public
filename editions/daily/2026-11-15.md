# Pleading AI output infringement turns out to need specifics

Judge Lee has found Concord Music's complaint against Anthropic lacking in specificity on infringing output from Claude, and has dismissed the direct copyright infringement claim against Dario Amodei with leave to amend.

[REPORTED] Concord may now file another amended complaint, and the parties have filed a joint status report proposing a schedule for it, according to Edward Lee's commentary on ChatGPT Is Eating the World. The output allegations, the part of the case about what Claude actually produced, must be pleaded again in more detail.

Concord has leave to amend, and so does the claim against Amodei, so nothing here ends the case. It is a US federal ruling on how the complaint is written, not a finding on whether Claude infringes.

The Verdict's view: Output is the harder ground for rights holders, because they must point to what the model produced, and a buyer's exposure to a supplier's output risk will stay theoretical until a complaint survives.

Ask each model supplier whether its indemnity covers claims about outputs, not only training.

[ANNOUNCED] Syntheia says the wave of MCP connectors from DMS, contract and research vendors is partly a distribution play, and that a thin wrapper around existing software does not redesign the work. In its own blog post the vendor writes that AI assistants let users move from A to K in one prompt, with MCPs needed for the remaining steps. Syntheia sells in the document automation category, so this is a supplier describing its own market.

The Verdict's view: A vendor selling redesigned workflows has reason to call connectors thin, but its test is fair: a connector earns its place only if it removes a step a lawyer used to take.

https://syntheia.io/blog/mcps-get-us-halfway-there

[REPORTED] Runway AI has pleaded both First Amendment and Copyright Clause defences to Ace Cam's DMCA anti-circumvention claim, according to Edward Lee's commentary. The commentary notes that including a defence does not mean it will be pursued, but that it preserves the point for later in the litigation. The case is in the US District Court for the Southern District of New York, before Judge Lewis Kaplan.

The Verdict's view: A defence pleaded to preserve a point is a marker, not a strategy, and the signal will be whether Runway presses it in motion practice.
