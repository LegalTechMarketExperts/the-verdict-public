# Mary Technology's fact tools arrive in Harvey by connector, with the price still unstated

*Mary Technology has announced a connector that brings its fact-investigation tools into Harvey; the release says what they do but not what they cost or which firms get them.*

[ANNOUNCED] Mary Technology, a Sydney and Austin-based legal AI start-up, has announced a connector that brings its fact-investigation tools into Harvey, the legal AI platform. Mary announced it on 21 October; LawFuel carried the release on 24 October. It is an integration of existing tools rather than a launch of new ones, and it extends Mary's work with Harvey into investigation for litigation teams. The release sets out what the tools do: weigh evidence for and against allegations, test witness accounts against documents, trace payments in financial records, flag contradictions and gaps, keep an audit history and propose updates when new documents arrive. It gives no pricing and names no firms.

The Verdict's view: A tool arriving inside a platform you already use can bypass a fresh buying decision, which is exactly when to ask which contract and data terms govern it.

Edward Lee's commentary at ChatGPT Is Eating the World notes a new copyright suit by owners of some Elvis songs against Anthropic, ABG EPE IP LLC et al. v. Anthropic PBC, filed 23 October in the Northern District of California. It describes this as the eighth AI music copyright suit Adams & Reese has filed this year, and puts the running total of AI copyright suits at 157; both counts are the commentator's own and no docket or independent tally confirms them.

The Verdict's view: A filing is not a finding and a tally is not a docket, so cite the case, not the count.

https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/10/24/copyright-owners-to-elvis-songs-sues-anthropic-total-157-suits-v-ai-cos

Legal Tech Monitor carries a commentary by Stephen Embry, first published in Above the Law on 14 October, arguing that surveys of lawyers' AI use overstate adoption. Embry cites a Visier survey of around 1,000 US employees across industries, not lawyers, in which 48 per cent said they had exaggerated their AI use or expertise to colleagues or managers; he notes himself that the study was not aimed at legal. He adds that audience shows of hands at conferences tell a different story.

The Verdict's view: A sceptic's show of hands is no better evidence than a survey, and both need to say who was asked and what was counted.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/ai-use-in-law-firms-theres-reality-and-theres-what-management-wants-it-to-be

Legal Tech Monitor also carries a commentary by Stephen Embry, a former defence lawyer, also published in Above the Law, on the US defence bar. The author argues that personal injury contingency fee firms hold an advantage with AI, and that defence firms may need to change their approach to keep pace.

The Verdict's view: The argument turns on fee structure, so a firm paid by the hour should test it against its own incentives before agreeing or dismissing it.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/the-defense-bar-in-the-age-of-ai-running-a-red-queens-race

[CONFIRMED] LawFuel reports that Joanne Vengadesan, a Malaysian-born technology partner at Penningtons Manches Cooper in London, has been elected chair-elect of Multilaw, a global network of independent law firms, at its conference in Prague; she takes a two-year term as chair from September 2027, succeeding Paul Kirton of Macpherson Kelley. The firm's own statement of 19 October confirms the election.

The Verdict's view: A chair-elect's term starts in eleven months, so for now the story records where a network is placing technology expertise, not what it will do with it.
