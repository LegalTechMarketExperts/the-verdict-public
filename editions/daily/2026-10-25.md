# Today's AI claims outrun the evidence behind them

*LawFuel reports that Mary Technology has put fact-investigation tools inside Harvey, but nothing yet says what they do, what they cost or who gets them.*

[REPORTED] LawFuel reports that Mary Technology, an Australian legal AI start-up, has launched fact-investigation tools inside Harvey, the legal AI platform. The move extends Mary's existing integration with Harvey into investigation work for litigation teams. No detail is given on what the tools do, how they are priced, which jurisdictions or firms have access, or whether either company has announced it. [VERIFY: source text unavailable; check against Harvey or Mary announcement]

The Verdict's view: A tool arriving inside a platform you already use can bypass a fresh buying decision, which is exactly when to ask which contract and data terms govern it.

https://lawfuel.com/legal-ai-tool-mary-brings-fact-investigation-tools-to-harvey

Edward Lee's commentary at ChatGPT Is Eating the World notes a new copyright suit by owners of some Elvis songs against Anthropic, ABE EPE LLC v. Anthropic, in the US. It describes this as the eighth AI music copyright suit Adams & Reese has filed this year, and puts the running total of AI copyright suits at 157, a count that is the commentator's own. [VERIFY: case filing and the 157 total against the court docket]

The Verdict's view: A filing is not a finding and a tally is not a docket, so cite the case, not the count.

https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/10/24/copyright-owners-to-elvis-songs-sues-anthropic-total-157-suits-v-ai-cos

Legal Tech Monitor republishes a commentary arguing that surveys of lawyers' AI use overstate adoption. The author cites a Visier study reporting a figure of 48 per cent of respondents; what that share refers to is not stated here. The author adds that audience shows of hands tell a different story. [VERIFY: the 48 per cent figure and the Visier study source]

The Verdict's view: A sceptic's show of hands is no better evidence than a survey, and both need to say who was asked and what was counted.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/ai-use-in-law-firms-theres-reality-and-theres-what-management-wants-it-to-be

Legal Tech Monitor also republishes a commentary, first published in Above the Law, by a former defence lawyer on the US defence bar. The author argues that personal injury contingency fee firms hold an advantage with AI, and that defence firms may need to change their approach to keep pace.

The Verdict's view: The argument turns on fee structure, so a firm paid by the hour should test it against its own incentives before agreeing or dismissing it.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/the-defense-bar-in-the-age-of-ai-running-a-red-queens-race
