# Lower finance costs mend Dye & Durham's loss, not its revenue

*Dye & Durham says its net loss narrowed to $11.1m while revenue fell 12.4 per cent: a repair, not yet a recovery.*

[ANNOUNCED] Dye & Durham says revenue for the three months to 30 September 2026 fell 12.4 per cent to $94.9m, or 8 per cent excluding the disposed Credas business. Net loss narrowed to $11.1m from $38.3m, and adjusted EBITDA was $46.6m.

Interim CEO Todd Schulte says the company cut operating costs by about 17 per cent to match cyclical revenue softness. The release says the narrower net loss mainly reflects a $26.3m fall in finance costs, to $19.6m. The figures are the company's own, and adjusted EBITDA is a measure it defines.

The Verdict's view: A loss narrowed mainly by lower finance costs is a repair, not a recovery, because revenue excluding Credas still fell 8 per cent.

Ask any supplier in cost-cutting mode which support and development commitments are protected in writing.

[ANNOUNCED] Orrick says Japan's Ministry of Finance promulgated implementing regulations for its revised FEFTA foreign investment screening regime on 16 September 2026. The amended regime takes effect on 4 January 2027, and prior notification for ICT investments applies to investments made from 3 February 2027. Foreign investors in Japanese ICT businesses, and the deal teams advising them, are the people affected. Orrick says AI infrastructure software, in-house models and RAG tools are in scope, while user-interface and API applications generally are not. The rules are made, not proposed; this is information, not legal advice.

The Verdict's view: Whether a target's AI sits on the infrastructure side of that line will decide whether a filing is needed, which makes architecture a legal question.
