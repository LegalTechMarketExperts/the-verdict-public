# Paying law firms for their emails puts privilege on the table

Above the Law reports that an unnamed technology company is paying law firms for access to their emails, and client privilege is the stake.

Above the Law reports that a technology company has launched a programme to pay law firms for their emails. The outlet says AI labs cannot scrape this data, which it presents as the point of the scheme. The report does not name the company or set out the terms. [VERIFY: identify the company and confirm programme terms from primary source]

Our read: the pitch is scarcity. Data the labs cannot scrape is worth more to them. It is also the category where client confidentiality and privilege decide whether a firm may sell at all. One outlet's report is not a market, and no firm is named as taking part. Before any client email leaves the building, ask who decides what counts as privileged.
