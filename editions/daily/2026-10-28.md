# Hidden AI labels on human-written documents deserve a buyer's attention

Greg Lambert reports that Lexis Protégé tags typed Word files as AI-generated, and says a week passed without a public vendor statement.

[REPORTED] Writing on 3 Geeks and a Law Blog, Greg Lambert reports from his own testing that Lexis Protégé exports label Word documents as AI-generated, including files the user typed. He describes five hidden document properties and says a week of internal discussion passed without an announcement from the vendor. The account rests on Lambert's own testing; he says a Lexis representative acknowledged on 26 October that the change should have been explained more clearly, but no public statement has been issued and LexisNexis has not confirmed the behaviour.

The Verdict's view: A wrong provenance flag is a disclosure problem in miniature, so test what your own AI tool writes into a document you typed yourself before a client or court asks.

https://geeklawblog.com/2026/10/lexis-protege-is-labeling-your-downloads-ai-generated-even-the-ones-you-typed-yourself.html

[CONFIRMED] The Solicitors Regulation Authority has published penalties totalling £44,800 for anti-money laundering breaches in England and Wales, covering decisions issued since its August round-up, according to Today's Conveyancer. Moss Solicitors LLP received the largest, £18,738, after a desk-based review found failures. The report breaks off before the full list of failures.

The Verdict's view: A desk-based review needs no inspector on site, so a firm's file evidence has to be ready without notice.

https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/firms-pay-penalties-44800-latest-round-aml-sanctions

[ANNOUNCED] Harvey says Latham & Watkins is collaborating with it to develop AI tools for fund formation documents, and Latham's AI leaders say the work fits the firm's wider strategy for the technology, according to Harvey's 27 October announcement. No financial terms or timetable are stated.

The Verdict's view: A named practice and a named vendor is testable in a way a firm-wide licence is not; the first matter-level results will say more than the announcement.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/latham-partners-with-harvey-to-develop-fund-formation-tools

[REPORTED] At ClioCon, Clio chief executive Jack Newton said lawyers are slow to adopt technology because they ask hard questions, according to Canadian Lawyer. Above the Law's write-up, headlined Doubling Down On Unification, says unification was the keynote's theme, and Canadian Lawyer says Clio also unveiled products, features and an acquisition. The acquisition is Page, a US legislative and regulatory tracking platform; terms were not disclosed.

The Verdict's view: Hard questions are a fair description of procurement; unification will be judged by whether the products ship as one, not by the keynote.

https://canadianlawyermag.com/news/general/lawyers-often-slow-to-adopt-new-tech-because-they-ask-hard-questions-clio-ceo/394852

[ANNOUNCED] Elite, the law firm financial operations vendor formerly part of Thomson Reuters, says an analysis of five of its own advisory assessments finds firms can identify an average of 1,648 hours a year of savings by modernising financial operations. The sample is five firms chosen by the vendor, the dollar figure uses an illustrative $200 hourly rate, and Elite itself calls the figures identified opportunities rather than audited savings.

The Verdict's view: An average of five vendor-selected assessments is a sales figure, not yet a benchmark.

https://elite.com/insights/news/law-firms-reclaim-1648-hours-financial-operations-modernization?hsLang=en
