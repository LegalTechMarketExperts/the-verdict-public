# Courts will not cut a bill on AI alone

The US Tenth Circuit upheld a $114,663.11 fee award to Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck, so a challenge to a bill on AI grounds needs specific entries behind it.

[REPORTED] The US Tenth Circuit has upheld a $114,663.11 fee award to Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck after Flux Drive's trade secrets suit was dismissed, Above the Law reports. Flux Drive argued that the firm's use of AI and online research tools should have cut the fees. The panel said it pointed to no specific inflated billing entry.

Judge Carolyn McHugh's opinion, covered by the ABA Journal citing Reuters, notes that AI may reduce research time but lawyers must still verify their authorities. The panel also upheld the dismissal.

The Verdict's view: a general complaint that AI should have made the work cheaper did not move this panel, so the argument shifts to individual time entries, where here the burden sat with the client.

Advice: make sure time entries show who checked AI-assisted authorities.

[ANNOUNCED] Clio's 2026 Legal Trends Report says 85 per cent of surveyed legal professionals report some AI adoption at their firms, and projects billable utilisation to rise 3.1 percentage points in 2026, the largest one-year increase in the report's history. Clio also says 64 per cent of AI-using respondents report a positive effect on revenue, up from 36 per cent in 2025.

That puts figures on the claim Above the Law questioned this week. Its writer, relayed by Legal Tech Monitor, spoke to Clio at the conference and suggests the trend may not be long-term.

The Verdict's view: rising utilisation suits firms that sell hours, but it is also the figure a client will cite when asking where the saved time went.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/11/lawyers-are-billing-more-time-as-ai-adoption-grows-according-to-2026-clio-legal-trends-report

[REPORTED] The Legal Ombudsman's budget is set to rise 6.9 per cent to £22.8m in 2027-28 under draft Office for Legal Complaints plans, Legal Futures reports, while the levy on lawyers should fall as case fees rise. Complaints are forecast at 21,210. Case fees would be £200, £750 or £1,500 by stage, with £400 for missing the eight-week final response deadline. The plans are drafts for England and Wales, not yet in force.

The Verdict's view: the £400 charge puts a price on slow complaint handling, and a forecast of 21,210 complaints makes it a cost to plan for.
