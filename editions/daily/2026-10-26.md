# AML compliance improves, on the regulator's own evidence

The Solicitors Regulation Authority says fewer firms are failing anti-money laundering rules, but the reporting does not say what "partially compliant" covers.

[CONFIRMED] The Solicitors Regulation Authority says the number of firms found non-compliant with anti-money laundering rules in England and Wales fell almost 10 per cent this year. It reports that 78 per cent of firms were either compliant or partially compliant, and that the proportion of non-compliant firms also declined. The figures come from the regulator's own review, as reported by Today's Conveyancer; the report does not state the starting figure or what "partially compliant" covers.

The Verdict's view: A regulator describing its own review is a primary source, but "partially compliant" carries much of the 78 per cent, and compliance vendors will quote the headline without it.

https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/sra-reports-continued-improvement-aml-measures

[REPORTED] The SME & Boutique Law Firm Alliance has urged the prime minister to halt a proposed scheme to take interest on lawyers' client accounts, which it calls a stealth tax, according to the Law Society Gazette. The group hints at legal action if the government proceeds. The scheme is proposed for England and Wales, not in force, and the report does not give its terms or timetable.

The Verdict's view: Firms cannot plan treasury or client-account systems around an unsettled scheme; this is a story for finance directors before it is one for technologists.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/solicitors-urge-prime-minister-to-halt-law-firm-stealth-tax/5128329.article
