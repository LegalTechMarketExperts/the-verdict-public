# AML compliance improves, on the regulator's own evidence

The Solicitors Regulation Authority says fewer firms are failing anti-money laundering rules, but "partially compliant" carries most of the good news and its definition is loose.

[CONFIRMED] The Solicitors Regulation Authority says the proportion of firms found non-compliant with anti-money laundering rules in England and Wales fell from 32.4 per cent to 21.9 per cent, on 937 inspections and desk-based reviews in the year to 5 April 2026. It reports that 78 per cent of firms were either compliant or partially compliant; the split is 206 compliant, 525 partially compliant and 206 not compliant. The figures come from the regulator's own annual report, as reported by Today's Conveyancer. Today's Conveyancer does not define "partially compliant"; the SRA does, loosely, as some controls needing improvement alongside good practice.

The Verdict's view: A regulator describing its own review is a primary source, but "partially compliant" carries much of the 78 per cent, and compliance vendors will quote the headline without it.

https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/sra-reports-continued-improvement-aml-measures

[REPORTED] The SME & Boutique Law Firm Alliance has urged the prime minister to halt a proposed scheme to take interest on lawyers' client accounts, which it says is widely described as a stealth tax, according to the Law Society Gazette. The group hints at legal action if the government proceeds. The scheme is proposed for England and Wales and not in force: the Ministry of Justice consulted in January 2026 on taking 75 per cent of interest on pooled client accounts and 50 per cent on individual accounts, and the outcome is awaited.

The Verdict's view: Firms cannot plan treasury or client-account systems around an unsettled scheme; this is a story for finance directors before it is one for technologists.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/solicitors-urge-prime-minister-to-halt-law-firm-stealth-tax/5128329.article

[CONFIRMED] Draftwise says Jones Day will deploy Ontonomi, a legal ontology platform, to connect institutional knowledge across the firm, and that this supports better-informed decisions. The announcement is dated 26 October and quotes Jones Day's global chair of AI and innovation, Giles Elliott; Jones Day issued its own release the next day.

The Verdict's view: A named firm lends a vendor credibility, but "deploy" says nothing yet about users, matters or results.

https://draftwise.com/blog/jones-day-partners-with-draftwise-to-deploy-ontonomi-legal-ontology-across-the-firm

[ANNOUNCED] Perfect Portal has launched a feature that allows quoting within InfoTrack, so law firms using both systems can manage new business without switching platforms, according to a Perfect Portal item carried by Legal Futures. The item says there are no additional fees for the feature; no customers are named.

The Verdict's view: Small integrations rarely make headlines and often decide whether staff use a tool at all.

https://legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/perfect-portal-and-infotrack-bring-new-business-management-closer-together

[CONFIRMED] The Council for Licensed Conveyancers says its latest sentiment tracker finds home buyers' confidence at around half the level seen at the start of the year, as reported by Today's Conveyancer. The regulator's survey is the basis for the figure; the article gives 110 respondents for one question and does not describe the method.

The Verdict's view: Confidence is not instructions, so conveyancing firms should wait for volumes before buying or cutting capacity.
