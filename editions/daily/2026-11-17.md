# Crosby says lawyers no longer review a fifth of its work

*Crosby, the New York AI law firm, says a $5m Testudo policy covers its AI agents and that it has removed lawyer oversight from tasks making up about 20 per cent of its work.*

[ANNOUNCED] Crosby says it has removed lawyer oversight from tasks that make up about 20 per cent of its work, which it calls paralegal tasks, and expects 15 agents per lawyer by the end of 2026. Non-Billable reports the claims from Crosby's own post, which says the $5m Testudo policy covering its AI agents took effect on 3 November.

Earlier this month the policy was the story, with Crosby saying it believes it is the first law firm to obtain liability cover for its agents. The development is what Crosby now says it supports: agents doing tasks that no lawyer checks.

The Verdict's view: Crosby is selling the absence of review, so the test is the first error that reaches a client and who answers for it, not the 15-agents target.

Ask any supplier that says it has removed review which tasks it counts as paralegal work, and who decides.

[REPORTED] The Legal Services Consumer Panel has urged the SRA not to abandon its plan to separate compliance officer and management roles at firms, the Law Society Gazette reports. In England and Wales the SRA has paused the rule, which would have applied to firms with turnover above £600,000 or more than £2m of client money, after smaller firms objected. Nothing is in force, so affected firms have no new duty from it yet.

The Verdict's view: The panel is arguing for a safeguard while the pause answers a cost objection, so a higher threshold is likelier than repeal.
