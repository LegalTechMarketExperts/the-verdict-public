# Harvey keeps buying, and buyers should read their contracts

*Harvey's purchase of Aida, its fifth deal this year, shows how quickly the ownership of a legal AI supplier can change.*

[REPORTED] Harvey has acquired Aida, an AI startup, in its fifth deal this year, according to Nonbillable's reporting. Legal Tech Monitor also carried the story. Nonbillable places the purchase within a wider buying spree among legal technology companies. No price or terms are stated. [VERIFY: confirm deal count and date against Harvey or Aida statement]

The Verdict's view: five deals in a year make a model vendor into a consolidator, and customers of an acquired tool gain a new owner without being asked; what that does to roadmap and price is the open question for a law firm buyer.

https://nonbillable.co.uk/news/harveys-latest-legal-ai-buy-adds-to-acquisition-wave

[CONFIRMED] Sir Geoffrey Vos, Master of the Rolls, opened the Law Society of Scotland's Annual Conference with a keynote on how AI will change the skills the legal profession needs; the judiciary's published text is dated 29 October 2026. Separately, The Law Society Gazette reports that he is retiring, with the Lady Chief Justice calling him the most technologically capable holder of the 800-year-old office. It gives no retirement date or successor. [VERIFY: confirm retirement date; Carr named only as Lady Chief Justice in the report]

The Verdict's view: the office's stance on technology now depends on a successor nobody has named, so do not assume the tone continues.

https://judiciary.uk/speech-by-the-master-of-the-rolls-are-human-lawyers-a-thing-of-the-past

[REPORTED] Outlets differ on whether Weil, the Wall Street firm, has collapsed, is winding down or faces closure. Legal Cheek reports around 30 trainees seeking new firms [VERIFY: trainee count from one outlet; check with CLLS]. The Law Society Gazette says the City of London Law Society is ready to help UK trainees and junior lawyers, and gives no timing for any collapse [VERIFY: status of Weil collapse is unconfirmed]. Above the Law reports associates are offered ninety days of pay and a bonus if they stay [VERIFY: check pay terms and bonus details against the internal message or further reporting].

The Verdict's view: whatever the firm confirms, the practical test is how cleanly clients' files and matters can move, which is a records and systems question before it is a recruitment one.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/stricken-weils-juniors-in-limbo/5128391.article

[CONFIRMED] The High Court, in England and Wales, has held void an £11m claim brought by solicitors who took an assignment of their client's cause of action, according to Legal Futures. The court relied on a 242-year-old rule that bars solicitors with conduct of litigation from taking such assignments.

The Verdict's view: any assigned-claim or funding structure, however modern its tooling, meets that rule first.

https://legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/court-voids-claim-brought-by-solicitors-assigned-clients-cause-of-action

[ANNOUNCED] Relativity says a Redgrave LLP study found its aiR Review located more responsive documents with less attorney time than a managed review team. The same post introduces Close Calls, an interactive review-skills game. [VERIFY: vendor promotion of a third-party study; check Redgrave report]

The Verdict's view: a vendor's promotion of a third-party study shows what the vendor chose to show; ask for the Redgrave report itself, with its sample and error rates, before the claim reaches a pitch deck.

https://relativity.com/blog/close-calls
