# Harvey keeps buying, and buyers should read their contracts

*Harvey's purchase of Aida, its fifth deal this year, shows how quickly the ownership of a legal AI supplier can change.*

[ANNOUNCED] Harvey has acquired Aida, an AI sales execution startup, in what its own post of 29 October calls its fifth acquisition of the year, after Lume, Hexus, Benchmark and Guardrails AI. Nonbillable and Legal Tech Monitor carried the story, and Nonbillable places the purchase within a wider buying spree among legal technology companies, noting that Legora has also made five deals in 2026. No price or terms are stated.

The Verdict's view: five deals in a year make a model vendor into a consolidator, and Legora's matching count suggests a pattern rather than an outlier; customers of an acquired tool gain a new owner without being asked, and what that does to roadmap and price is the open question for a law firm buyer.

https://www.harvey.ai/blog/aida-joins-harvey
https://nonbillable.co.uk/news/harveys-latest-legal-ai-buy-adds-to-acquisition-wave

[CONFIRMED] Sir Geoffrey Vos, Master of the Rolls, opened the Law Society of Scotland's Annual Conference with a keynote on how AI will change the skills the legal profession needs; the judiciary's published text is dated 29 October 2026. Separately, The Law Society Gazette reports that he is retiring, with the Lady Chief Justice, Baroness Carr, calling him the most technologically capable holder of the office. He steps down on 31 October, a date announced in January; Sir Colin Birss, Chancellor of the High Court, succeeds him, as announced in March.

The Verdict's view: the office's stance on technology now passes to Sir Colin Birss, who chaired the CJC's AI working group, so the tone is likelier to continue than to change.

https://judiciary.uk/speech-by-the-master-of-the-rolls-are-human-lawyers-a-thing-of-the-past

[REPORTED] Weil, the Wall Street firm, has said its management committee recommended an orderly wind-down, subject to a partner vote; outlets describe this as collapse or closure. Legal Cheek cites an unnamed source for a 31 December London closure and estimates around 30 trainees in London seeking new firms; neither Weil nor the City of London Law Society has given a figure. The Law Society Gazette says the City of London Law Society is ready to help UK trainees and junior lawyers. Above the Law reports an internal message offering up to ninety days of salary and benefits, and a bonus for those who stay to 1 January 2027.

The Verdict's view: whatever the partners vote, the practical test is how cleanly clients' files and matters can move, which is a records and systems question before it is a recruitment one.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/stricken-weils-juniors-in-limbo/5128391.article

[CONFIRMED] The High Court, in England and Wales, has held void an £11m claim brought by solicitors who took an assignment of their client's cause of action, according to Legal Futures. The court relied on a 242-year-old rule that bars solicitors with conduct of litigation from taking such assignments.

The Verdict's view: any assigned-claim or funding structure, however modern its tooling, meets that rule first.

https://legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/court-voids-claim-brought-by-solicitors-assigned-clients-cause-of-action

[ANNOUNCED] Relativity says a Redgrave LLP study found its aiR Review located more responsive documents with less attorney time than a 24-person review team using Relativity's own active learning tool. The same post introduces Close Calls, an interactive review-skills game.

The Verdict's view: a vendor's promotion of a third-party study shows what the vendor chose to show; read the Redgrave working paper, public since June and not peer reviewed, and note that aiR's precision was lower than the team's before the claim reaches a pitch deck.

https://relativity.com/blog/close-calls
