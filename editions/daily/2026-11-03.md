# The NCSC's AI warning is really about cheap technology

*The National Cyber Security Centre says UK critical national infrastructure faces AI-powered attacks within two years, and puts the blame on 'tech on the cheap'.*

[CONFIRMED] NCSC chief executive Richard Horne, launching the State of the Cyber Nation report, has warned that UK critical national infrastructure faces AI-powered attacks within two years. According to the NCSC, 'tech on the cheap' has left organisations exposed to AI tools that uncover and exploit long-standing security weaknesses. The agency calls for urgent investment in resilience. This is a warning from the agency, not a rule in force.

The Verdict's view: the sentence that matters is the one about cheap technology, which turns old systems into a purchasing decision rather than an IT chore. Few law firms are critical infrastructure; some of their clients are.

https://ncsc.gov.uk/news/cyber-chief-uk-facing-ai-powered-attacks-on-critical-infrastructure-in-two-years

[REPORTED] Artificial Lawyer reports that Hg, the private equity owner of Litera, and Anthropic are to invest in an 'AI Transformation' venture, alongside some angel investors. Artificial Lawyer puts Hg's assets under management at $110bn. The sum, the name and who will run it are not stated; the remit is AI adoption and AI-native product build on Claude, with a European focus, launching later this year.

The Verdict's view: a platform owner and a model maker committing to invest together signals direction, not yet a product.

https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/11/03/litera-owner-hg-anthropic-invest-in-ai-transformation-venture

[REPORTED] Non-Billable reports that AI-focused firms Vetta, Scissero and Crosby are bidding for the technology, IP and lawyers of Weil, Gotshal & Manges, whose management committee has recommended an orderly wind-down, subject to a partner vote. Scissero's chief executive and Vetta's founder are on the record. Law360 Pulse separately reports an unnamed AI law firm weighing private equity backing as it bids for Weil's technology and talent. None of the reports gives terms.

The Verdict's view: two bidders on the record and no terms is still not a sale; Weil naming a buyer, or the partner vote, is what settles it.

https://nonbillable.co.uk/news/ai-law-firms-circle-weil-assets

[ANNOUNCED] Carina Negreanu, who leads Microsoft's legal vertical, says in an Artificial Lawyer interview that Copilot for Legal is built for the long term and could potentially reach hundreds of thousands of lawyers worldwide. The reach is Microsoft's own projection, from one interview.

The Verdict's view: 'potentially' carries the weight in that sentence.

https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/11/03/copilot-for-legal-al-interview-carina-negreanu-microsoft

[REPORTED] Legal IT Insider reports that Co-Flo has launched AI-powered matter management inside iManage, combining time, WIP and billing data from the practice management system (Aderant only at launch) with real-time matter progress. It is aimed at law firms.

The Verdict's view: finance beside progress is only as useful as the practice management data feeding it, and at launch that means Aderant.

https://legaltechnology.com/co-flo-brings-matter-management-and-financial-intelligence-into-imanage

[REPORTED] Legal Tech Monitor reports that attorneys say a New Jersey bill would bring AI chatbots under products liability law, which they warn could 'open the floodgates' to claims. It is proposed, not in force, and the report gives no bill number or vote timing.

The Verdict's view: read it as a hint about where supplier liability will be argued, not as law.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/11/njs-proposed-liability-for-ai-chatbots-may-lsquoopen-the-floodgates-attorneys-say
