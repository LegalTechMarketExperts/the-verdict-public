# Two legal AI rivals launch Intake; the name is the lesser point

Legora and Harvey each unveiled a product called Intake within a day of one another, and the buyer's question is what each does with the work it routes.

Legal Cheek reports that Legora unveiled Intake on Tuesday: requests arrive through Slack, and an agent asks for missing information, assigns tasks and prepares work for review. Artificial Lawyer reports that Harvey followed on Wednesday with its own Intake, a front door that routes legal requests to agents, pre-built solutions or lawyers, with early access from October and Carvana, GE Aerospace and Dentsu as design partners. Neither report gives commercial terms.

Our read: the name clash is a bad afternoon for two marketing teams. The pattern matters more. Two leading legal AI vendors are now competing over how in-house work is received and sorted, not only how it is drafted. For law firms, that is where instructions may start to be triaged. A front door is only as good as the rules behind it, and no launch report describes them. Ask who audits the routing decisions an intake tool makes.

**Legora and Laurel link time capture to AI work.** The companies say lawyers will record time automatically while working in Legora, with Laurel capturing the work as time entries, according to LawSites. They add that they will later develop measures of Legora's value to firms. Our read: the later measure is the one to watch, because whoever defines value writes the renewal case.

https://lawnext.com/2026/10/legora-and-laurel-partner-to-enable-automatic-time-capture-will-later-develop-measures-of-legoras-value-to-firms.html

**Opus 2 buys Bundledocs.** Opus 2 has acquired Bundledocs, the companies announced on 1 October; Legal Futures carried the release this week. The deal positions Opus 2 in commercial disputes software and is its third acquisition in five years, after Uncover in 2025; 23 Bundledocs staff in Cork join. Terms are undisclosed and no completion date is given.

https://legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/opus-2-acquires-bundledocs-to-advance-its-position-as-the-leader-in-commercial-disputes-software

**SixFifty ships under Paychex.** LawSites reports SixFifty's first major release since the Paychex acquisition: an agentic HR compliance feature. Release details are not fully available. Our read: "agentic" remains a label until the feature list says which steps run without a person.

https://lawnext.com/2026/10/in-first-major-release-since-paychex-acquisition-sixfifty-launches-agentic-hr-compliance-feature.html

**Courts and Tribunals Bill reaches the Lords.** In England and Wales, the revamped bill, designed to cut the Crown court backlog, cleared the Commons on 13 October and goes to the Lords; it is proposed law only, according to The Law Society Gazette. The report gives no date for the Lords stages or the vote count.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/revamped-courts-and-tribunals-bill-passes-crucial-hurdle/5128214.article

**Judiciary backs cautious AI use.** Legal Cheek reports that the judiciary in England and Wales endorses AI with strict safeguards and robust testing, but draws the line at robot judges. The document is the judiciary's Position Statement on the Judicial use of AI, led by Sir Colin Birss, the lead judge for AI; it builds on the December 2023 guidance. Our read: safeguards and testing are the wording suppliers will be asked to meet.

https://legalcheek.com/2026/10/judiciary-gives-ai-cautious-thumbs-up-but-draws-line-at-robot-judges

**Caroline Corby named SRA chair.** The Law Society Gazette reports she will succeed Anna Bradley on 23 November; she chairs the Crown Prosecution Service and the Professional Standards Authority, the oversight body for health and care regulators, and has pledged to lead change. The SRA has confirmed the appointment. Our read: a new chair is a reason to reread assumptions about the pace of regulatory technology policy.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/sra-names-new-chair-with-pledge-to-lead-change/5128211.article
