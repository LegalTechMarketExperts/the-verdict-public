# Weil's troubles are being blamed on AI without evidence

Weil, Gotshal and Manges says its management committee has recommended an orderly wind-down, with a partner vote expected within days, and the AI explanations on offer are running ahead of the facts.

[CONFIRMED] Weil, Gotshal and Manges, a firm of about 1,200 lawyers, is winding down. A Weil spokesperson says the management committee has recommended an orderly wind-down, partners are expected to vote within days, and clients will be served over the coming months. Above the Law's Morning Docket cites the Wall Street Journal saying AlixPartners is assisting. The ABA Journal, citing Reuters, reports the firm hired restructuring lawyers from Davis Polk after merger talks with Willkie Farr failed. Legal Cheek reports the London office is expected to close by 31 December.

Artificial Lawyer argues AI did not cause the planned dissolution, and adds no new facts on the firm's finances.

The Verdict's view: this is a partnership story first; treat "AI did it" as a claim until the firm's own figures appear.

https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/10/29/ai-didnt-cause-the-weil-crisis

[ANNOUNCED] Harvey has announced a design partnership with Latham and Watkins on an AI tool for fund formation, with both parties quoted; Legal Cheek carries the story as part of the firm's wider technology push. Harvey's announcement sets out the scope (fund documents including LPAs, side letters and subscription documents) but no commercial terms or ownership.

The Verdict's view: co-building is a stronger signal than a licence, but with no terms, no ownership and no customer beyond Latham it is a project, not yet a product.

https://legalcheek.com/2026/10/latham-teams-up-with-harvey-to-build-new-ai-tool

[ANNOUNCED] Anthropic has extended its legal push with a gallery of ready-made legal artifacts, announced on 28 October, following the May launch of Claude for Legal. Artificial Lawyer, reporting the move, says the company is preparing for an IPO; Anthropic's own post does not mention one. Anthropic lists the workflows but gives no pricing.

The Verdict's view: another model maker courting lawyers matters to legal AI vendors and their customers, but with the workflows listed and no price or uptake, the test is who pays for it.

https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/10/28/anthropic-increases-legal-vertical-push-with-artifacts

[REPORTED] Law360 Pulse reports that a Denver law firm has been sued over a delayed data breach notice, in a proposed class action filed in the District of Colorado on 27 October. The firm, the claimant, the breach and the notice dates are not stated in the part of the report that is open to read, and the complaint has not been seen.

The Verdict's view: late notification is where a security incident becomes a liability claim, so ask who decides when your clients are told, and by when.

https://law360.com/pulse/legal-tech/articles/2543600

[CONFIRMED] The Council for Licensed Conveyancers states that from 29 October 2026 a reduced Lifetime ISA withdrawal charge applies to first-time buyers purchasing property above the £450,000 price cap. The charge falls from 25% to 20%. This follows the October 2026 Budget, applies UK-wide, and is in force; the CLC's notice is addressed to the licensed conveyancers it regulates in England and Wales. It is information, not legal advice.

The Verdict's view: a rule that takes effect on the day of its notice tests how fast quoting software is updated; conveyancing teams should know the answer for theirs.
