# Legora's $8.8bn price tag is a bet, not a result

*Legora's $550m raise at an $8.8bn valuation raises the stakes for legal AI platforms, though the outlets differ on who led the round.*

[REPORTED] Sifted reports that Legora has raised $550m at an $8.8bn valuation, backed by Atreides Management and JP Morgan. Legal IT Insider, relaying the company's 21 October announcement, calls it a Series E led by Atreides, with the money going to US and international expansion and the Legora aOS platform. Legal Cheek places it in a race among legal AI platforms. Sifted's independent reporting is the strongest source; it gives no round stage, and Legal Cheek gives no round size or investors. [VERIFY: check round size, valuation and investors against Legora's announcement] [VERIFY: confirm lead investor and valuation] [VERIFY: check round size and lead investors against The Lawyer and company statement]

The Verdict's view: a valuation measures investors' expectations, not your outcomes, so ask the platform vendor to state in writing what the money buys for firms of your size and what it does to your price.

https://sifted.eu/articles/legora-funding-round-series-e-legal-tech

[REPORTED] The Law Society Gazette reports that the SRA has dropped a blanket rule separating the compliance officer for legal practice (COLP) from the compliance officer for finance and administration, and will target higher-risk firms instead. This is England and Wales; the report does not say how higher risk will be judged.

The Verdict's view: combined roles remain the default, but a firm that looks risky may be told to split them.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/sra-kicks-colp-separation-plans-into-the-long-grass/5128307.article

[REPORTED] Above the Law reports that the Tennessee Supreme Court has kept the state's ban on private equity owning law firms without tightening limits on outside investment, so private equity can still take a financial interest in a firm's business. No ruling date is given. [VERIFY: check the ruling date and the exact scope of the court's order]

The Verdict's view: the ownership line holds while the investment line stays open, so the question for firms is what a financial interest may lawfully include.

https://abovethelaw.com/2026/10/private-equity-cant-own-tennessee-law-firms-but-it-can-still-get-a-piece-of-the-business

[ANNOUNCED] Autologyx says its Model Context Protocol connector is live in Harvey's Connector Library, as reported by Legal IT Insider, letting shared customers link Harvey with Autologyx's tools; Mary Technology's new capabilities are announced alongside. Harvey's own undated library page says it lists security-reviewed connectors to iManage, NetDocuments, SharePoint and PitchBook, plus Custom MCP. [VERIFY: confirm connector scope and Mary Technology role]

The Verdict's view: a listing shows a connector exists, not that it honours your document permissions, so ask to see it do so on a live matter first.

https://legaltechnology.com/autologyx-mary-technology-bring-new-capabilities-into-harvey

[ANNOUNCED] Everlaw says 86 per cent of respondents to its In-House Innovation Report use GenAI for legal work, according to Doug Austin's commentary on eDiscovery Today. The sample size and what counts as use are not stated. [VERIFY: Check the 86 per cent figure in Everlaw's report]

The Verdict's view: a vendor-run usage figure shows attitude until it says how often, and on which matters.

https://ediscoverytoday.com/2026/10/21/in-house-innovation-report-from-everlaw-artificial-intelligence-trends
