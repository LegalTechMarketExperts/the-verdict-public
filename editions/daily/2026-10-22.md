# Legora's $8.8bn price tag is a bet, not a result

*Legora's $550m raise at an $8.8bn valuation raises the stakes for legal AI platforms, and the company says Atreides led.*

[ANNOUNCED] Legora says it has raised a $550m Series E at an $8.8bn post-money valuation, led by Atreides Management, with J.P. Morgan among the new investors. Sifted reports the round, and Legal IT Insider, relaying the company's 21 October announcement, says the money goes to expansion in North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific and to the Legora aOS platform. Legal Cheek places it in a race among legal AI platforms. Legora's own release gives the round stage and full investor list.

The Verdict's view: a valuation measures investors' expectations, not your outcomes, so ask the platform vendor to state in writing what the money buys for firms of your size and what it does to your price.

https://legora.com/newsroom/legora-raises-550-million-series-e-at-8.8-billion-valuation-to-accelerate-growth
https://sifted.eu/articles/legora-funding-round-series-e-legal-tech

[REPORTED] The Law Society Gazette reports that the SRA has paused its blanket rule separating the compliance officer for legal practice (COLP) from the compliance officer for finance and administration, and is moving to supervise higher-risk firms proactively instead. This is England and Wales; the January 2027 start will not happen, and the report does not say how higher risk will be judged.

The Verdict's view: combined roles remain the default for now, and a firm identified as higher risk should expect to be asked for assurances on the independence of its compliance function rather than an order to split the roles.

https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/sra-kicks-colp-separation-plans-into-the-long-grass/5128307.article

[REPORTED] Above the Law reports that the Tennessee Supreme Court has kept the state's ban on private equity owning law firms without tightening limits on outside investment, so private equity can still fund a firm's back office through a management services organisation. The order is dated 2 October 2026 and also opened law school accreditation to bodies other than the ABA and set up three task forces.

The Verdict's view: the ownership line holds while the investment line stays open, so the question for firms is what a financial interest may lawfully include.

https://abovethelaw.com/2026/10/private-equity-cant-own-tennessee-law-firms-but-it-can-still-get-a-piece-of-the-business

[ANNOUNCED] Autologyx says its Model Context Protocol connector is live in Harvey's Connector Library, as reported by Legal IT Insider, letting shared customers link Harvey with Autologyx's tools; Mary Technology separately announced a Harvey integration the same day. Harvey's own undated library page lists both, alongside security-reviewed connectors to iManage, NetDocuments, SharePoint and PitchBook, plus Custom MCP.

The Verdict's view: a listing shows a connector exists, not that it honours your document permissions, so ask to see it do so on a live matter first.

https://legaltechnology.com/autologyx-mary-technology-bring-new-capabilities-into-harvey

[ANNOUNCED] Everlaw says 86 per cent of respondents to its In-House Innovation Report use GenAI for legal work, according to Doug Austin's commentary on eDiscovery Today. The sample is 300 US in-house legal professionals, surveyed with Wakefield Research; what counts as use is not defined.

The Verdict's view: a vendor-run usage figure shows attitude until it says how often, and on which matters.

https://ediscoverytoday.com/2026/10/21/in-house-innovation-report-from-everlaw-artificial-intelligence-trends

Announced: they said it themselves. Reported: a named outlet reported it. Confirmed: a document or a named source on the record backs it.

Capital is plentiful at the top of legal AI; evidence for the buyer is still scarce.

When a vendor's valuation rises, what do you ask for in your contract that you did not ask for last year?
