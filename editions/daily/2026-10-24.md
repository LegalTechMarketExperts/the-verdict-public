# Courts set the AI timetable; vendors supply the numbers

A near five-hour hearing in Concord Music v. Anthropic leaves more to come on 30 October, while Everlaw and 8am offer figures that have yet to meet a second source.

[REPORTED] Judge Eumi Lee of the US District Court for the Northern District of California heard nearly five hours of argument on summary judgment in Concord Music v. Anthropic I on 21 October, according to Edward Lee's blog, ChatGPT Is Eating the World. The blog says she questioned counsel for both sides, and that a second hearing, by videoconference, will continue the Daubert hearing on 30 October at 1pm. It reports no ruling.

The Verdict's view: A hearing is not a ruling, so buyers have nothing to price yet; decide now which outcome would make you reopen an AI supplier's terms, and leave them alone until it arrives.

https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/10/23/judge-lee-concludes-marathon-hearing-on-summary-judgment-in-concord-music-v-anthropic-i-sets-2d-hearing-to-continue-daubert-hearing-on-oct-30-1-pm

[ANNOUNCED] Everlaw says PGH Petroleum, a GAI company, cut first-pass review time by an estimated 80 per cent on a single 10,000-document production using Everlaw AI, and found deeper insights. The figure comes from the vendor's own customer story and has not been independently checked.

The Verdict's view: Ask what the 80 per cent was measured against, the same documents reviewed without AI or an estimate, before it reaches a business case.

https://everlaw.com/resources/success-stories/pgh-petroleum-uses-everlaw-ai-to-reduce-first-pass-review-time-case-study

[ANNOUNCED] Practice management and payments vendor 8am says data from more than 60,000 mostly US firms on its MyCase and LawPay platforms shows invoiced revenue per firm up 5 to 8 per cent year on year while hourly rates held flat at three of four firm sizes; 48 per cent of firms bill only by the hour. Legal IT Insider covered the report, which compares the first halves of 2025 and 2026 and reflects 8am's own customers.

The Verdict's view: The billable hour looks entrenched, but a billing vendor's mostly US customers are not the UK market.

https://legaltechnology.com/8am-data-law-firms-bill-more-without-raising-rates-as-hourly-rates-hold-firm

[REPORTED] Legaltech News reports day-two takeaways from the KM&I for Legal conference in New York (21 to 22 October), quoting Will Gaus, chief knowledge management and innovation officer at Troutman Pepper Locke, which has republished the piece. The coverage deals with responsible AI-assisted coding and fostering AI innovation; wider detail of the session is not given.

The Verdict's view: Knowledge teams that write code with AI take on software risk, and "responsible" needs a written standard before it is more than a slogan.

https://troutman.com/insights/kmi-for-legal-day-two-take-aways-responsible-vibe-coding-fostering-ai-innovation
