# Courts set the AI timetable; vendors supply the numbers

A near five-hour hearing in Concord Music v. Anthropic leaves more to come on 30 October, while Everlaw and 8am offer figures that have yet to meet a second source.

[REPORTED] Judge Eumi Lee of the US District Court for the Northern District of California heard nearly five hours of argument on summary judgment in Concord Music v. Anthropic, according to Edward Lee's blog, ChatGPT Is Eating the World. The blog says she questioned counsel for both sides, and that a second hearing will continue the Daubert hearing on 30 October at 1pm. It reports no ruling. [VERIFY: check hearing date and docket against court record]

The Verdict's view: A hearing is not a ruling, so buyers have nothing to price yet; decide now which outcome would make you reopen an AI supplier's terms, and leave them alone until it arrives.

https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/10/23/judge-lee-concludes-marathon-hearing-on-summary-judgment-in-concord-music-v-anthropic-i-sets-2d-hearing-to-continue-daubert-hearing-on-oct-30-1-pm

[ANNOUNCED] Everlaw says PGH Petroleum, part of GAI, cut first-pass review time by 80 per cent using Everlaw AI, and found deeper insights. The figure comes from the vendor's own customer story and has not been independently checked. [VERIFY: 80 per cent figure is vendor-supplied; check with customer]

The Verdict's view: Ask what the 80 per cent was measured against, the same documents reviewed without AI or an estimate, before it reaches a business case.

https://everlaw.com/resources/success-stories/pgh-petroleum-uses-everlaw-ai-to-reduce-first-pass-review-time-case-study

[ANNOUNCED] Billing and payments vendor 8am says its year-on-year data shows nearly half of law firms bill more without raising hourly rates, as covered by Legal IT Insider. The full figures and sample are not available, and the data reflects 8am's own customers. [VERIFY: check the exact percentage and sample size in the full 8am report]

The Verdict's view: The billable hour looks entrenched, but a billing vendor's customers are not the market.

https://legaltechnology.com/8am-data-law-firms-bill-more-without-raising-rates-as-hourly-rates-hold-firm

[ANNOUNCED] Troutman Pepper Locke has published its takeaways from day two of its KM&I for Legal event, citing Will Gaus, its chief knowledge management and innovation officer, who was quoted in coverage of the 22 October 2026 Legaltech conference. The post covers responsible AI-assisted coding and fostering AI innovation; wider detail of the session is not given. [VERIFY: check Gaus quote and event date against the Legaltech report]

The Verdict's view: Knowledge teams that write code with AI take on software risk, and "responsible" needs a written standard before it is more than a slogan.

https://troutman.com/insights/kmi-for-legal-day-two-take-aways-responsible-vibe-coding-fostering-ai-innovation
