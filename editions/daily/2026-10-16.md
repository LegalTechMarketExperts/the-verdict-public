# Microsoft arrives in legal through the door lawyers already use

Microsoft has launched Copilot for Legal, according to Artificial Lawyer, and the stake is who owns the lawyer's desktop.

Artificial Lawyer reports that Microsoft has launched Copilot for Legal, a multi-capability offering it describes as a move into the legal sector. The article, dated 15 October 2026, frames the launch as a significant step for lawyers who use Word. It says the plugins roll out to Microsoft 365 Copilot commercial licence holders; it gives no pricing or client-data terms.

Our read: distribution is the story, not capability. A tool inside Word needs no procurement campaign, which is exactly why it deserves scrutiny. Vendors selling on drafting alone will feel it first; firms will feel it as quiet adoption before any contract is signed. Ask Microsoft where its legal offering ends and your existing licence begins.

The Law Society Gazette reports that Sir Geoffrey Vos, Master of the Rolls, has called for an international debate on good practice in AI use in judicial and arbitral decision-making. He warns that cross-border enforcement is at risk. The judiciary has published the text of his International Chamber of Commerce speech. Our read: enforcement rests on trust in how a decision was reached, which is a client question as much as a judicial one.

Law360 Pulse Legal Tech reports that a cyber incident has halted bankruptcy court noticing across the United States, affecting the Bankruptcy Noticing Center, which issues notices for federal bankruptcy courts. The US Courts said on 15 October that the incident hit contractor BAE Systems, which runs the centre, and that notice production, the BNC websites and the National Creditor Registration Service were down; no cause or restoration date was given. Our read: a court's back office is part of every litigator's operational risk.

Control Risks announced on 15 October that it will spin off its Discovery and Data Insights and Forensics businesses into a new stand-alone company, to trade initially as Control Risks – Compliance, Investigations & eDiscovery, with Beringer Capital investing and Control Risks keeping a stake. Georges Sabongui, formerly chief executive of CloudNine, will lead it. Closing is expected in the fourth quarter of 2026; no value is given. LawSites carried the story, syndicated by Legal Tech Monitor.

Lineal announced on 14 October that it has acquired e-discovery and forensics company Forentec, also reported by Legal Tech Monitor. Lineal says the Zurich-area firm adds digital forensics, cyber security and open-source intelligence capability and gives it a Swiss base for EMEA; terms are not disclosed. Our read: e-discovery is being reshaped by separation and acquisition in the same week.

The SRA confirmed that it is seeking views on proposed updates to the solicitor competence framework, in a consultation published on 8 October 2026. This is England and Wales, proposed and not in force; it affects solicitors and their employers. It closes on 3 December 2026 and covers technology and AI use, supervision, wellbeing and ethics, plus the SQE functioning legal knowledge; final text is due in April, in force from September. Our read: treat a consultation as direction, not requirement.

https://sra.org.uk/news/news/releases/competence-framework
