# Weil's data has bidders, but no buyer yet

*Scissero, Crosby and Vetta want pieces of Weil, Gotshal & Manges, and who may sell a collapsing firm's client data is now a live question.*

[REPORTED] Crosby, which has raised about $85m, has bid for the trademark, brand and some unspecified internal data of Weil, Gotshal & Manges, LawFuel reports, relaying Artificial Lawyer and TechCrunch. Scissero has expressed interest in its technology, IP and data, and Vetta wants precedents and lawyers.

Since Tuesday's item on the bidders, Legal Tech Monitor has reported that at least two AI-native firms have bid, and asks whether internal law firm data is an asset an outside buyer can acquire. LawFuel says no asset transfer has been announced, and cites a $10m bid for airline data as a benchmark.

The Verdict's view: Client confidentiality, not price, will decide what a collapsing firm can sell, and a bidder paying for matter data may be buying a duty rather than an asset.

[REPORTED] The New York State Bar Association's AI committee has issued a report, adopted by the bar's governing body, saying AI software costs are overhead and can be passed on as a disbursement only when tied to a specific client matter and disclosed, ABA Journal reports, citing Law360. Lawyers need not tell clients about AI used for purely administrative tasks, provided fees are unaffected. President Taa Grays says the guidance is meant to help attorneys use AI ethically.

The Verdict's view: Guidance against billing for time saved hands clients a ready question, and hourly-rate firms will feel it first, though it binds only New York.

https://abajournal.com/news/article/ny-bar-tells-lawyers-not-to-bill-for-time-saved-by-ai

[ANNOUNCED] After CEO Josh Baxter's intelligence-layer pitch last week, NetDocuments' chief product officer Dan Hauck has supplied the product detail in a LawNext podcast recorded at Inspire in Orlando. He describes a redesigned platform, a Microsoft 365 add-in, real-time co-authoring, generally available tabular review, document generation and redlining, and a beta MCP connector with Legora. He says the legal context graph, launched earlier this year, maps relationships among matters, documents and people.

The Verdict's view: A beta connector to Legora suggests document management vendors expect to sit beside AI tools rather than beat them, but a beta is a direction of travel, not yet a working integration.
