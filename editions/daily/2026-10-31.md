# Legal AI platforms widen their range faster than the evidence

Harvey has acquired the sales execution company Aida, its fifth purchase of 2026, Harvey announced on 29 October; no price or terms are given.

[ANNOUNCED] Harvey says it has acquired Aida, an AI-native sales execution platform co-founded by Piyush Prahladka and Atish Das Sarma, its fifth acquisition this year after Benchmark in July and Guardrails in September. The founders and their search and machine learning engineers join Harvey's product and engineering team; no price or terms are given. Law360 Pulse followed the announcement on 30 October.

The Verdict's view: Sales tooling sits a long way from drafting and research, so read this as a vendor widening its range, not as proof that the range fits a law firm.

https://law360.com/pulse/legal-tech/articles/2544160

[REPORTED] The ABA Journal reports that CrowdStrike's 2026 Legal Threat Landscape Report, published 28 October, counts 455 legal entities named on extortion leak sites in the year to August 2026, up 39% on the year, with CrowdStrike's Adam Meyers telling Reuters that more attackers are using agentic tooling. The report is US-focused.

The Verdict's view: Leak-site listings are a measure of extortion, not of breaches, and the sample is American; translate both before the figure moves a UK security budget.

https://abajournal.com/news/article/law-firms-facing-increasing-ai-fueled-cyber-attacks-new-report-says

[REPORTED] LawFuel reports that Gibson Dunn is poised to take about 40 lawyers from Weil as London departures accelerate. The group is Weil's London private funds team, eight partners and about 30 counsel and associates, first reported by Reuters on 29 October, and the move follows Weil management's recommendation to wind the firm down, pending a partner vote. Neither firm has confirmed.

The Verdict's view: When a team walks, the know-how and matter history it built stay with the firm only if someone planned for it.

https://lawfuel.com/gibson-dunn-poised-to-take-40-weil-lawyers-as-london-exodus-accelerates

[ANNOUNCED] Latham & Watkins says the FCA has set out good and poor practice on firms' use of AI, published 19 October, covering the Consumer Duty, governance and operational resilience. In Latham's account it shows how the existing UK framework applies, rather than adding a new rule, and the FCA says it creates no new requirements.

The Verdict's view: Regulated clients will measure their suppliers against this, so expect its language to turn up in your next AI questionnaire.

https://lw.com/en/insights/fca-issues-guidance-for-firms-on-their-use-of-ai

[ANNOUNCED] NetDocuments chief executive Josh Baxter says, in a LawNext podcast carried by Legal Tech Monitor, that the document management system is becoming a firm's intelligence layer. He adds that lawyers increasingly work inside Claude, Harvey, Legora and ChatGPT.

The Verdict's view: A document vendor naming itself the centre is describing its own role, not a settled architecture.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/lawnext-podcast-netdocs-ceo-josh-baxter-on-why-the-dms-is-becoming-the-law-firms-intelligence-layer

[ANNOUNCED] Mary Technology says its litigation fact-management tool is now available inside Anthropic's Claude through an MCP connector, announced 28 October and following a Harvey connector earlier in the month; commercial terms are not stated.

The Verdict's view: Availability on an assistant is a listing, not a deployment; find out what the integration actually does.

https://lawfuel.com/legal-tech-news-mary-technology-now-available-on-claude
