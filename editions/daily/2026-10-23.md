# A federal judge loses a case over AI errors

*The Fifth Circuit has taken a judge off a case over AI-linked errors in his orders, and the opinion is now public.*

[CONFIRMED] The Fifth Circuit has vacated a preliminary injunction issued by Judge Henry Wingate of the Southern District of Mississippi and directed that the case be reassigned, after finding his earlier orders contained what it called extraordinarily grave errors linked to AI use. Law360 Pulse and Legal Tech Monitor both carried the story. The case is Jackson Federation of Teachers v Fitch, a challenge to Mississippi's anti-DEI law; the errors sat in a 2025 temporary restraining order and its replacement, and the court vacated the preliminary injunction built on them. The opinion does not name the tool; Judge Wingate has said a clerk used Perplexity.

The Verdict's view: Accountability for AI output is now being enforced against the decision-maker, not only the filer. A firm that files in court should be able to show, for any document, which drafts a model touched.

https://law360.com/pulse/legal-tech/articles/2541559
https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/25/25-60496-CV0.pdf

[REPORTED] LawSites reports that LexisNexis Protégé is now live inside EvenUp, the AI platform for personal injury lawyers, letting users query case law using facts from their case records. Bob Ambrogi's report notes the two announced the integration on 1 September and that it went live earlier this month. Legal Tech Monitor also carried it. It requires subscriptions to both products; pricing is not stated.

The Verdict's view: Research is becoming a feature inside other products, so the licence you hold matters more than the screen you use.

https://lawnext.com/2026/10/lexisnexis-protege-is-now-live-in-evenup-letting-pi-lawyers-query-case-law-using-facts-from-their-case-records.html

[CONFIRMED] The Council for Licensed Conveyancers has issued guidance urging members in England and Wales to act on a critical PHP security update. The flaw could let attackers run commands on affected web servers. The regulator notes that PHP is widely used and may be built into other software. This is guidance, not a rule.

The Verdict's view: "Built into other software" is the operational point; the exposure may sit in a supplier's product nobody thinks of as PHP.

https://clc-uk.org/visual-composer-20278

[ANNOUNCED] Cooley says on its website that it advised Blacksmith, a code-testing infrastructure company, on a $66m Series C at a $1bn valuation, announced on 21 October. The round was led by Sequoia Capital, with Premji Invest, Peak XV Partners, GV and Y Combinator; total funding stands at $125m.

The Verdict's view: Blacksmith is not a legal tech vendor, so the interest here is the adviser credit, not the company.

[REPORTED] ABA Journal reports, citing 8am data, that hourly billing significantly outpaces flat fees at small and midsize US law firms, despite predictions of the billable hour's death. The data covers more than 60,000 firms on 8am's MyCase and LawPay platforms in the first half of 2026: 48% bill only by the hour, 10% only by flat fee, 42% use both.

The Verdict's view: Pricing models move more slowly than conference talk suggests, so test any fixed-fee AI business case against your own billing mix.
