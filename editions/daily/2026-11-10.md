# Addleshaw Goddard's Harvey deal rests on what the two build

Addleshaw Goddard says it will roll Harvey out to more than 3,400 professionals from January and co-develop tools with it.

[ANNOUNCED] Addleshaw Goddard says its 80-member Innovation Group tested several platforms over three months before it chose Harvey. The firm says the joint work has already produced Review Tables. Its announcement, relayed by Solicitors Journal, covers staff in Europe, the Middle East and Asia.

It follows last week's report that Harvey held preliminary meetings with Latham advisers over a potential listing. A named firm, a stated test period and a start date are firmer evidence of how the product is bought than talk of a listing.

The Verdict's view: co-developing with a supplier buys a say in its roadmap as well as a licence, and the value lies in who owns what the two build, not in the rollout date.

Advice: settle who owns jointly built tools, and what happens to them if you leave, before you sign.

https://solicitorsjournal.com/sjarticle/addleshaw-goddard-forms-ai-collaboration--?category=none

[ANNOUNCED] Blackletter, a US AI-native law firm, says it has raised a $6m seed round led by Lerer Hippeau, Artificial Lawyer reports. The firm says it has seven lawyers and works on M&A, finance and other complex matters. Co-founder and CEO Jonathan Rosenbluth says the old law firm model serves the firm and its senior partners, not clients. Artificial Lawyer says the firm refuses billable hours and has built its own operating system, BlackletterOS.

The Verdict's view: a seven-lawyer firm without billable hours tests a pricing model more than a technology, and repeat clients on complex deals will say more than the size of the round.

https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/11/09/blackletter-bags-6m-for-transactional-new-mod

[CONFIRMED] The government introduced the Courts and Tribunals Bill on 25 February 2026, according to HM Courts and Tribunals Service and the Ministry of Justice. For England and Wales, it proposes ending defendants' right to elect Crown Court trial in triable-either-way cases, creating a Bench Division and allowing judge-alone trial in some complex fraud cases. It is a proposal: the Commons has passed it, the Lords began committee stage on 10 November, and nothing is in force. The page was updated on 9 November with a new impact document.

The Verdict's view: the technology effect is indirect and later, since workflows built around the election decision change only if the bill survives as drafted.

Advice: criminal and fraud teams should model the change now and hold any spending until the text settles.
