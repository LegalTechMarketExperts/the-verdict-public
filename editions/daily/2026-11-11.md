# UK firms choose rival legal AI vendors as risk gets priced

Addleshaw Goddard has chosen Harvey and Fieldfisher has chosen Legora, while Crosby says its AI paralegals now carry $5m of insurance.

[ANNOUNCED] Addleshaw Goddard says it will roll Harvey out to more than 3,400 lawyers and staff across Europe, the Middle East and Asia, and co-develop AI services with it. Fieldfisher says it has chosen Legora for its 1,400 lawyers and business services staff. Legal Cheek adds that Hogan Lovells Cadwalader works with both vendors.

Harvey and Legora were both reported on 6 November to be weighing a listing, Harvey through early talks with Latham & Watkins and Legora through sounding out Wall Street; each now has a large UK client to cite.

The Verdict's view: a rollout is not usage, and a seat count is the number a prospectus would quote; the vendor whose customers publish active use will be believed first.

Ask for weekly active users, not licensed seats, before you read a headcount as adoption.

https://legalcheek.com/2026/11/addleshaws-and-fieldfisher-pick-rival-legal-ai-giants-harvey-and-legora-as-adoption-race-heats-up

[ANNOUNCED] Crosby, which Law360 Pulse Legal Tech describes as a hybrid AI law firm, has bought a $5m malpractice insurance policy. Crosby says the cover is with Testudo, underwritten by Lloyd's of London, and covers its new paralegal agents. Crosby says it believes this is the first time a law firm has obtained liability insurance for the outputs of its AI agents.

The Verdict's view: an insurer pricing agent output matters more than the first-of-its-kind claim, and the policy wording, not the $5m limit, will show a buyer what is covered.

https://law360.com/pulse/legal-tech/articles/2548170
https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/11/10/crosby-insures-paralegal-agents-interview

[ANNOUNCED] Blue J, the Toronto tax research platform, says it has raised $176m in a Series E led by JMI Equity at a valuation above $700m. It says the money will fund its Workflows agentic AI and expansion into the UK and other markets, and that more than 6,500 organisations use it, as relayed by LawNext.

The Verdict's view: money is going to narrow, domain-deep tools while the general platforms court law firm seat counts, and the UK entry will test whether tax teams pay for depth.

https://lawnext.com/2026/11/tax-research-platform-blue-j-raises-176m-to-expand-its-agentic-ai-and-reach-new-markets.html
