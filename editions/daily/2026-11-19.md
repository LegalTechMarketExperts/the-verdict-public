# MPs want a single regulator; clients want their AI savings

The Commons justice committee backs a national legal service and a single regulator, while named senior in-house lawyers tell firms to share AI savings.

[ANNOUNCED] The Commons justice committee's access to legal services report calls for a national legal service to coordinate advice, modelled on NHS 111, Legal Futures reports. MPs back an interest on lawyers' client accounts scheme in principle, though not as the Ministry of Justice proposed. They also urge AI-assisted public legal information, built on a public repository to reduce hallucination risk.

The committee also says the regulatory framework does not serve access to justice and needs a new settlement. It says a single regulator could simplify the system, which is the model Professor Stephen Mayson proposes. It also flags technology use by unregulated providers and weak Legal Services Board oversight. This follows the Lord Chancellor's National Legal Service pitch at last weekend's Bar Council conference. These are recommendations for England and Wales, not law, and nothing changes for regulated firms yet.

The Verdict's view: The NHS 111 comparison will draw the headlines, but a single regulator is the idea that would redraw a firm's compliance map, and the committee says only that it could simplify the system.

Ask your COLP to list which current authorisation conditions a single regulator would change, before anyone asks you.

[REPORTED] OpenAI has opened its Astra for Law configuration to all ChatGPT Enterprise customers on request, moving it to the GPT-6.2 Astra base model, LawSites reports. OpenAI says it scored 20.8 per cent on the LAB-AA benchmark and 53.8 per cent on the Vals AI Legal Research Bench. LawSites notes that these are OpenAI's own claims. It also notes that the 46 per cent September baseline OpenAI cited for the Vals bench differs from the 54 per cent it reported at the September launch.

The Verdict's view: A general-purpose lab selling straight to enterprise legal teams will test legal-specific vendors on price, but scores that moved since September are a reason for a trial of your own.

[REPORTED] Senior in-house lawyers at Compass Group, Lloyds Banking Group and News UK told the Chambers Connects event in London that firms must pass AI efficiencies on to clients or risk losing work, Legal Futures reports. Pricing transparency and junior lawyer staffing were also discussed. Costs lawyers heard a similar demand last week; now it has named buyers behind it.

The Verdict's view: Once named clients say it from a stage, the saving becomes a pricing conversation, and a relationship partner who arrives without a number will be handed one.
