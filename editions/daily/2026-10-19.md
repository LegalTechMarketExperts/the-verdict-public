# The gap at the top of UK law is widening

*PwC's annual law firm survey, reported by Legal Futures, finds the top 100 firms still growing while the gap between the top 10 and the rest widens.*

Legal Futures reports that PwC's annual law firm survey finds the top 100 firms still growing, with the gap between the top 10 and the rest widening. PwC warns that disruptive competitors may be a strategic blind spot for large firms. The reporting gives no growth figures and names no competitors.

Our read: both findings can be true at once. The firms pulling ahead have the most capacity to invest, and they are also the ones being told to watch their flank. For a firm outside the top 10, the practical point is to benchmark technology spend against peers of its own size, not against the leaders.

**Microsoft joins the legal AI contest.** The Lawyer reports that Microsoft launched Copilot for legal on 15 October, adding to the contest among large technology companies to build legal AI products. **Returns are now the question.** The Lawyer examines how law firms are looking for returns after rolling out AI tools. Beyond the headline theme, the findings are not stated here. Our read: for many firms the question has arrived after the contract, which is the wrong order.

**Docusign on post-quantum signatures.** Docusign says, in a profile of engineer Peleg Atar, that it is bringing post-quantum cryptography to its digital signatures and platform seals. It says the "harvest now, decrypt later" threat makes this urgent before quantum computers arrive. This is the company's own account, and no date is stated. Our read: the exposure sits in long-lived archives of signed documents, which is where a supplier's date would matter.

https://docusign.com/blog/meet-the-engineer-peleg-atar

**Whiplash reforms under review.** In England and Wales, the Government has issued a call for evidence on the whiplash reforms, five years after they changed how lower-value road traffic accident claims are handled, according to Legal Futures. It is a call for evidence, not a proposal, and the closing date is not stated. Firms handling these claims are affected; our read is that nothing needs rebuilding yet.
