# A&O Shearman's $1.1bn AI plan is mostly lawyers' time

*A&O Shearman puts $1.1bn on a five-year AI strategy, and the firm says most of it is lawyers' time.*

[ANNOUNCED] A&O Shearman says it will commit $1.1bn to AI over five years, with most of the sum coming from each practice committing at least 3 per cent of its time to building AI tools and services, according to Legal Business, which carried the firm's announcement. Legal Tech Monitor puts it as around $1bn of lawyer hours and calls it among the largest law firm AI investments so far. Law360 Pulse Legal Tech says the firm frames the build as lawyer-led.

Every figure is the firm's own. City AM reports that the 3 per cent is of billable hours, while the firm's release says only that each practice will invest at least 3 per cent of its time. A rival weighing a similar plan will want to know how those hours were valued.

The Verdict's view: a number made largely of hours says more about the firm's priorities than its spend, and the test is whether the 3 per cent survives a busy quarter.

https://legalbusiness.co.uk/law-firms/ao-shearman-announces-new-ai-strategy-valued-at-1-1bn-over-five-years

[REPORTED] The government has accepted in full the recommendations of the Richard Lloyd review of legal services regulation, The Law Society Gazette reports. Justice minister Sarah Sackman backs a "reset" of the system. The Ministry of Justice is to develop a new framework by 2029, with implementation in the next parliament, and the government says AI and lawtech will be considered in a comprehensive review. The measure is proposed, not in force: regulated firms and chambers have nothing to change yet, and the Law Society and Bar Council have responded.

The Verdict's view: three more years under the present rulebook is the real news, and a firm waiting for the reset to settle how it governs AI will be waiting into the next parliament.

Write your AI supervision rules against today's regulation and treat 2029 as a revision.

https://lawgazette.co.uk/news/minister-promises-legal-regulation-reset-in-2029/5128445.article

[REPORTED] Clio is moving into the courts, LawSites and LawNext reports, with the hires of Pablo Arredondo and Bridget McCormack and the acquisition of Learned Hand, an AI start-up for judges. Bob Ambrogi's 50-state survey finds judicial AI policies vary widely: Massachusetts bans judges and court staff from using generative AI for legal research and writing, while New York is more permissive. The survey relies on ChatGPT and Claude checks as well as the author's own research.

The Verdict's view: a vendor selling to judges faces fifty rulebooks rather than one market, and where a state bars the use, the sale starts with a policy change, not a demo.

https://lawnext.com/2026/11/clio-wants-to-bring-ai-to-the-judiciary-but-50-state-survey-reveals-policies-differ-in-whether-they-allow-it.html
