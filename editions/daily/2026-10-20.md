# Research content is moving into the AI platforms, with claims attached

*Midpage says its research costs a tenth of Westlaw and Lexis and now feeds Harvey; the price claim is its own and the terms are not stated.*

[ANNOUNCED] Midpage, a US AI legal research platform, now connects to Harvey through an MCP connector, according to Legal Tech Monitor and Artificial Lawyer. Harvey customers can find and cite US case law, statutes and regulations from inside Harvey. Midpage says its service is ten times cheaper than comparable Westlaw and Lexis products and covers much of US case law. Both reports rest on the companies' own account; neither sets out commercial terms or how the integration works. [VERIFY: price claim is Midpage's own; check integration terms with Harvey.]

The Verdict's view: A tenth of the price is a claim until a customer shows an invoice, and the firmer point is that a research challenger has chosen to sit inside a drafting platform rather than fight it; ask whether a connector is a licensed data feed or a plain search link.

https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/10/19/legal-research-challenger-midpage-channels-data-into-harvey

[ANNOUNCED] Trellis says it has put its state trial court research data inside Microsoft 365 Copilot, reports Legal Tech Monitor, so attorneys can search successful motions and research judges and opposing counsel there. The report gives no price or licence terms. The Verdict's view: Research is becoming something lawyers reach from the assistant they already have open, which shifts negotiating weight to whoever owns the content.

https://legaltechmonitor.com/2026/10/trellis-brings-state-trial-court-research-to-microsoft-365-copilot

[REPORTED] LawFuel reports that Harvey will open an office in Tokyo. It gives no timing, staffing or scope. [VERIFY: confirm the Tokyo office announcement with Harvey or a primary source.] The Verdict's view: An office is not a customer base; Japan matters when named firms appear.

https://lawfuel.com/legal-tech-news-harvey-to-open-tokyo-office

[REPORTED] Commentator Edward Lee reports that Stris & Maher and Freedman Normand have filed a fifth near-identical copyright suit against Meta for book authors, 292 in total, and counts 150 copyright suits against AI companies. One complaint alleges infringement through torrenting and through scanning physical books. [VERIFY: confirm filings, author counts and the 150 total against court dockets.] The Verdict's view: Suit counts tell a buyer little until rulings arrive; volume is not merit.

https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/10/19/plaintiffs-firm-floods-docket-with-5th-near-identical-copyright-suit-v-meta-on-behalf-of-book-authors-292-authors-total

[REPORTED] Above the Law reports that a US judge called a $3,000 hourly rate "patently unreasonable", while noting that some Biglaw partners charge $4,000. The judge and case are not identified. [VERIFY: check judge, court and case name in the full article.] The Verdict's view: Fee justification is a courtroom question as well as a client one, and AI-assisted work will meet it too.

https://abovethelaw.com/2026/10/judge-says-a-3000-hourly-rate-is-patently-unreasonable
