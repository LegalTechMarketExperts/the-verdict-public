# Research content is moving into the AI platforms, with claims attached

*Midpage says its research costs a tenth of Westlaw and Lexis and now feeds Harvey; the price claim is a spokesperson's and no pricing is given.*

[ANNOUNCED] Midpage, a US AI legal research platform, now connects to Harvey through an MCP connector, according to LawSites and Artificial Lawyer. Harvey customers can find and cite US case law, statutes and regulations from inside Harvey. A Midpage spokesperson told Artificial Lawyer the service is ten times cheaper than comparable Westlaw and Lexis products and covers much of US case law; the figure does not appear in Midpage's own 16 October launch post. Both reports rest on the companies' own account. The connector sits in Harvey's Connector Library, is open to joint customers and needs a Midpage subscription; neither report gives pricing.

The Verdict's view: A tenth of the price is a claim until a customer shows an invoice, and the firmer point is that a research challenger has chosen to sit inside a drafting platform rather than fight it; ask whether a connector is a licensed data feed or a plain search link.

https://artificiallawyer.com/2026/10/19/legal-research-challenger-midpage-channels-data-into-harvey

[ANNOUNCED] Trellis said on 19 October that it has put its state trial court research data inside Microsoft 365 Copilot, so attorneys can search successful motions and research judges and opposing counsel there. The announcement gives no price or licence terms. The Verdict's view: Research is becoming something lawyers reach from the assistant they already have open, which shifts negotiating weight to whoever owns the content.

https://blog.trellis.law/

[ANNOUNCED] Harvey says it will open a Tokyo office in early 2027, naming Mori Hamada & Matsumoto and Nagashima Ohno & Tsunematsu among local customers; it gives no staffing figure. The 12 October announcement also lists Mitsubishi Corporation and a partnership with Legalscape, and places Tokyo alongside Sydney, Singapore and Bengaluru. The Verdict's view: Named firms before the office opens is the right order; the test is whether a Tokyo headcount follows them.

https://www.harvey.ai/blog/harvey-to-open-tokyo-office

[REPORTED] Commentator Edward Lee reports that Stris & Maher and Freedman Normand have filed a fifth near-identical copyright suit against Meta for book authors, 292 in total, and says the running tally of US copyright suits against AI companies has passed 150. The Winspear complaint, per Lee, alleges infringement through torrenting and through scanning physical books. The filings have not been checked against court dockets. The Verdict's view: Suit counts tell a buyer little until rulings arrive; volume is not merit.

https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/10/19/plaintiffs-firm-floods-docket-with-5th-near-identical-copyright-suit-v-meta-on-behalf-of-book-authors-292-authors-total

[REPORTED] Above the Law reports that Judge Beth Labson Freeman of the Northern District of California called a $3,000 hourly rate patently unreasonable in the $68m Google Assistant privacy settlement, cutting it to $1,600, while noting that some Biglaw partners charge $4,000. The Verdict's view: Fee justification is a courtroom question as well as a client one, and AI-assisted work will meet it too.

https://abovethelaw.com/2026/10/judge-says-a-3000-hourly-rate-is-patently-unreasonable
